STN LFP Time–Frequency — v1 vs v2

Reach-to-grasp (self-paced), 8 PD subjects, contralateral-hemisphere STN channel. Identical input data — the two pipelines differ only in method. Figures generated from F:\Projects\Parkinson_ReachGrasp\Reprocessing, R2025a + EEGLAB.

Headline: The two pipelines agree on the movement-related beta desynchronisation (ERD) — both show it, in every subject. They differ most where it matters scientifically: v2 recovers the post-movement beta rebound (PMBR), which v1 cannot show by construction. v2 is also far cleaner (less interpolation noise) and uses a controlled, interpretable scale.

1 · Grand-average time–frequency map

Both maps show the two velocity peaks of the task in the overlaid kinematic trace (peak-reach and peak-pull velocity) — a good sanity check that the warping is aligned. What differs:

  • Beta ERD (≈13–30 Hz drop, blue) is present in both, but in v1 it is a continuous smear across the whole window, whereas in v2 it localises into two pockets that sit under the two velocity peaks — the physiologically expected pattern.
  • Scale. v1 sets its colour limits from the data min/max, so outlier bursts compress the scale and most of the map sits in undifferentiated mid-green. v2's symmetric ±5 dB shows graded modulation: a broad, modest power increase (pink) with the beta dips standing out.
  • Axes. v1's x-axis is in seconds built from averaged latencies (a fictitious "average trial"); v2's is % of movement with explicit 0 % = onset / 100 % = offset markers — the correct frame for warped data. v1's frequency axis runs uncontrolled to ~80+ Hz (Morse default); v2 is fixed to [1,80] Hz.

2 · Phase-resolved power spectra (the decisive difference)

This is the single most important result. In v2, the orange Rest (post) trace rises to ≈ +1.5 dB in the 18–28 Hz band — the classic post-movement beta rebound. In v1 this signal is invisible: v1 has only four phases and folds the post-movement rest into the baseline, so "Rest" is pinned flat at zero by construction. If the PMBR matters to your question (it usually does in PD/STN work), v1 structurally cannot report it and v2 can.

Both agree on the movement effect — reach (green) and grasp (blue) show the deepest beta drop, pull (purple) milder. The magnitudes look very different (v1 ≈ −33 %, v2 ≈ −1.2 dB) but that is a units difference, not a disagreement (see caveat). Note also v1's much wider SEM bands: its three-stage interpolation injects between-subject variance that v2's single warp avoids.

3 · Per-subject example (wue05)

At the single-subject level the difference in noise is obvious. v1 is grainy and speckled with isolated gamma "hot pixels" (individual beta/gamma bursts surviving the linear interpolation), and the frequency axis stretches to ~160 Hz. v2 resolves a clean beta-ERD trough around each velocity peak. Same data, same channel — the texture difference is entirely methodological.

Caveat on comparing magnitudes. v1 reports percent change (P−bl)/bl·100 and v2 reports decibels 10·log10(P/bl); e.g. −33 % ≈ −1.7 dB and +1.5 dB ≈ +41 %. The traces are therefore not on the same axis — the meaningful comparison is the pattern (which effects appear, where, how cleanly), not the numbers. The two pipelines also use different baselines (v1: rest pre+post; v2: rest pre only), which is exactly why the PMBR survives in v2 and vanishes in v1.
Recommendation: use v2. It reproduces every effect v1 shows, adds the post-movement beta rebound that v1 structurally hides, and does so with less interpolation noise and an interpretable scale. The one thing to remember is that both pipelines time-warp, so neither preserves absolute latencies — read timing as "% of movement", not seconds.
